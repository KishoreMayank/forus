Maya Chen

  1. 1Connections in one line. “Synced 9:04 AM” is all staff need. Clicking it lists the four sources and what each is used for. A failed sync would turn this amber.
  2. 2Where the list comes from, in one line. Every patient here has treatment in their chart and nothing booked. There are only two states: in progress or booked. No progress bar, because nobody knows in advance how many steps a patient will take.
  3. 3In progress comes first, three facts per row. Who and what treatment, with which dentist; what’s going on now; and what happens next. The dot only changes when a person is involved (amber) or the patient asked to pause (hollow).
  4. 4Booked sits below, quieter. Same row shape, greyed names. Once someone is booked the only remaining step is a reminder. The appointment book opens from here when you need it.
  1. 7FAQ and chart, side by side. Pick a question and a real patient to see the reply that would go out, with each sentence tinted by its source. Staff can check a wording edit against a real case before saving it.
  2. 8Plain markdown files. Anyone at the desk can edit them. A line starting with “→ Ask the dentist” is a rule: the tool won’t answer that question itself. It offers a discussion instead.
  1. 9Three practice systems, named for what staff know. Patient charts, treatment history and scheduling each answer one question: who needs follow-up, is it finished, and when can they come in. Texting is how the tool reaches patients.
  2. 10“Writes: Nothing” is said out loud. Charts and history are read-only. Only Scheduling writes, and only the appointments patients choose. That’s the line an owner checks before signing off.
  3. 11A sync log in plain words. “Found 2 new treatment plans” ties the connection to the two new patients at the top of Patients, so staff can see where new patients came from.
  4. 12Each missing integration says what it would unlock. Insurance is linked to the costs.md draft, which explains why that FAQ isn’t live yet. Phone calls are marked planned, not offered.
  5. 13The calendar belongs to Scheduling. It opens as a pop-up from the Scheduling row, or from the Booked group on Patients. It’s a read-only view of the real appointment book, with follow-up bookings coloured.
  6. 14One filter, nothing else. “Booked by follow-up” fades the rest of the book. A “New” tag marks bookings made since you last looked, so a busy desk notices them without a notification.
